GPU Teaching Lab — Cost Summary

Prepared 24 August 2026, revised 27 August 2026  ·  illustrated for a class of 100  ·  all figures USD per month

Students get their own GPU notebook environment with a hard spending limit built in. Nobody can quietly run up a large bill: when a student reaches their limit, their machine is shut down automatically and they cannot start another one.

$30 Typical cost per student, per month
$80 Hard ceiling per student, per month
~$11 Shared platform cost for all 100 students

What a class of 100 costs

Item What it is Per month
GPU time 100 students × about 6 hours a week each $2,930
Student storage Saved work, kept between sessions, 56¢ each $56
Shared platform Web console, database, monitoring, web firewall — for the whole class $11
Expected monthly total $2,997

That works out to about $30 per student, per month.

98% of the bill is GPU time students actually use. Everything else — the platform, the accounts, the storage — comes to $67 a month for all 100 students. Costs are metered by the second, so a student who does not log in costs 56¢ rather than $30.

The worst case is bounded

This is the part that matters for planning: the exposure is capped by design, not by trust.

Scenario One month One trimester
Expected — normal use $2,997 $9,001

Budget we recommend setting aside — $26,000

The guardrails cost under 1% of the spending they control.

Running the limit-enforcement machinery for 100 students costs about $70 a month against an $8,070 ceiling — 0.87%. Even if every cost assumption is pushed the wrong way at once it reaches 3%. There is no realistic way for the platform itself to become a material cost; the only line that can move the bill is GPU hours.

Two honest caveats about the $80 limit.

The shutdown is not instantaneous. Usage is checked every five minutes, so a student can land a few cents above their limit — about 9 cents at the GPU machine we recommend, and 12 cents at the most expensive one we allow. Across all 100 students that is under $10. Earlier designs checked only a few times a day, which let an $80 limit drift to $89–97 — about $1,350 a month of drift across this class size. That is the problem the five-minute check solves.

The limit covers machine time, not saved files. A student's 56¢ of storage sits outside the $80, so the true worst case per student is $80.56 plus a few cents. Students are shown this wording on their own usage page rather than left to discover it.

Allow a little headroom above $80 per student; the $26,000 trimester budget covers it comfortably.

Five points worth knowing

  • The platform itself is essentially free, even at 100 students. Running the console, database, monitoring and the web firewall in front of the console costs about $11 a month for the entire class — $10 of which is fixed and does not change with class size. It grows by roughly one cent per student, so headcount is not what drives the bill.
  • Idle costs are negligible. Between trimesters, with no students enrolled, the lab costs about $10 a month — the fixed part of the platform — and an enrolled student who never logs in adds 56¢. There is no need to tear the lab down and rebuild it.
  • The main cost risk is a machine left running, and it is handled. A GPU notebook forgotten over a weekend would cost around $1,000 a month if nothing stopped it. Eight of them left running for a trimester would consume the entire $26,000 budget. The automatic shutdown is what turns that into a capped $80.
  • The choice of GPU is worth $736 a month at this class size. The machine we default to, ml.g6.xlarge, is both newer and about 20% cheaper per hour than ml.g5.xlarge — $1.1270 against $1.4100 per hour, on the list prices cited below. An $80 limit buys about 71 hours on it — roughly 2.7 hours a week across a trimester. The full comparison is in docs/COSTING.md.
  • Students can see their own spending. Each student has a page showing what they have used this month, how much of their limit is left, and roughly how many hours that buys them. It is the same figure the shutdown decision is made on, so there is nothing to dispute after the fact.
